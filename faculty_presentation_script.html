Project Title & Problem Statement

0:00 – 0:30 (30 sec)
What to Say (Word-for-Word)

"Good morning sir/ma'am. Our project is 'Explainable Hybrid Deep Learning-Based Intrusion Detection System for IoT Networks'.

IoT devices are easy to attack. They are also too weak to run heavy security software. So we want an intrusion detection system that is accurate, with few false alarms. It must be explainable, so it shows why it raised an alarm. And it must be lightweight, small and fast enough for an IoT gateway.

Our proposed model is a hybrid CNN-GRU, with SHAP explanations. Our main dataset is Edge-IIoTset: 2.2 million packets, Normal traffic plus 14 attack types."

Accurate Explainable Lightweight
🎯 Goal: State the three requirements we will check ourselves against.
2

First Result: 100%, and Why It Was Fake

0:30 – 1:10 (40 sec)
What to Say (Word-for-Word)

"Our first models were Random Forest, CNN, GRU and CNN-GRU. All of them scored 100% on Normal versus Attack. That was too good to be true. So we investigated instead of celebrating.

We found the cause. The dataset writes an empty field in two different ways. In the Normal captures, it is written as "0". In the attack captures, it is written as "0.0". After one-hot encoding, these become two different columns. So one column alone gives away the label.

For the DNS field, 1,613,798 Normal rows use "0", and 603,331 attack rows use "0.0". The MQTT fields have the same problem. Our early SHAP plots even hid it, because they cut the "_0" and "_0.0" from the feature names. So the 100% was a formatting shortcut, not real detection."

Show: results/figures/fig1_fake_vs_honest.png. The first bar is the fake score. The last bar is the final honest setting.

"0" vs "0.0" shortcut Investigated, not celebrated
🎯 Goal: Show we caught our own mistake. This is the strongest part of the story.
3

How We Fixed It

1:10 – 1:45 (35 sec)
What to Say (Word-for-Word)

"We fixed it in six steps.
One. We canonicalise the text, so "0" and "0.0" mean the same.
Two. A strict feature policy. We removed 7 per-packet identifiers, like TCP sequence numbers. They identify the capture file, not the attack.
Three. We removed all MQTT fields as a check, because every MQTT packet here is Normal.
Four. Automatic shortcut scans look for any one-column giveaway.
Five. Guards. Training and SHAP refuse mismatched data.
Six. A GPU pipeline on our laptop: about 8 seconds per epoch instead of 31.

Every comparison uses 3 random seeds. Thresholds come from validation data only."

"0" = "0.0" 7 identifiers removed Scans + guards GPU ≈8 s/epoch
🎯 Goal: Each fix has a reason. Say the reason, not just the step.

Honest Results on Edge-IIoTset & Robustness

1:45 – 2:15 (30 sec)
What to Say (Word-for-Word)

"Here are the honest test results, Normal versus Attack. Our tuned CNN-GRU gets a Macro-F1 of 0.858. Accuracy is 90.0%. False alarms are only 0.7%. Our XGBoost baseline gets 0.868.

Why It Stops at 0.86: The Detection Ceiling

2:15 – 2:45 (30 sec)
What to Say (Word-for-Word)

"So why does it stop at about 0.86? We measured it. The 1.55 million training packets contain only 11,122 distinct feature patterns. And 31.9% of attack packets look exactly like patterns that are mostly Normal.

Even a perfect model that memorises every pattern detects only 67.5% of attacks, at 0.5% false alarms. Our CNN-GRU detects 65.3%. XGBoost detects 67.3%. Both are at the ceiling. The missed attacks are not a model problem. The information is simply not in a single packet."

Limit of Packet Data & Second Dataset (CICIoT2023)

3:50 – 4:30 (40 sec)
What to Say (Word-for-Word)

"Packet data has one more limit. Telling the attack type apart is weak on Edge-IIoTset: XGBoost 0.429, CNN-GRU 0.337. Floods and scans are defined by packet rate. One packet cannot show rate. And this dataset has no timing for attack packets.

So we added a second dataset, CICIoT2023. It is flow-level, with rate and timing. Here we found a second hidden shortcut. The packet_count column is just the window size: always 10 for Benign and Recon, and 100 for DDoS, DoS and Mirai. We removed it. After the fix, the scan found no giveaway.

Results: Benign versus Attack, XGBoost 0.917, CNN-GRU 0.890. For 8 categories, XGBoost 0.752, CNN-GRU 0.675, double its Edge-IIoTset score. And SHAP now uses rate, as expected."

Show: results/figures/fig4_datasets.png.

Faculty Q&A Defense Simulator
Anticipated Questions
Q1: "Why not 100%? Your first models reached it."
Honest Answer to Deliver

"Sir/Ma'am, the 100% was fake. The dataset writes an empty field as "0" in Normal captures and as "0.0" in attack captures. After encoding, one column gave away the label. After we made "0" and "0.0" the same and removed the per-packet identifiers, the honest score is 0.858.

And 100% is not possible on this data. We measured that 31.9% of attack packets have exactly the same features as mostly-Normal patterns. Even a perfect lookup detects only 67.5% of attacks. So a 100% score here would itself be a warning sign."

Do NOT say: "Our model is 100% accurate." The 100% only appears as the fake result we found and removed.
Q2: "How do you know the model isn't still cheating?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"We have several independent checks.
One: we removed 7 per-packet identifiers, like TCP sequence numbers and checksums, because they identify the capture file.
Two: removing all MQTT fields changes almost nothing, 0.8579 to 0.8575. So the MQTT shortcut is not used.
Three: automatic shortcut scans, a single-feature test and a depth-3 tree, look for any one-column giveaway.
Four: SHAP shows the model uses TCP behaviour, and the removal test confirms it.
Five: 3 seeds agree, and thresholds are chosen on validation data only.

On CICIoT2023 the same scans found a second shortcut, the window size. After removing it, the best single feature reaches only 0.85, and the binary score barely moved, 0.9166 to 0.9165. We cannot prove no shortcut is left anywhere. But every check we know of now passes."

Q3: "Why do 35% of attacks get missed?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"Because those packets look exactly like normal ones. The 1.55 million training packets have only 11,122 distinct patterns. 31.9% of attack packets share their exact features with mostly-Normal patterns. A perfect lookup detects at most 67.5%. We detect 65.3%, so we are at the ceiling.

We can trade false alarms for detection. With a lower threshold chosen on validation, detection rises to 73.3%, but false alarms rise to 6.6%. The real fix is better data. On flow-level CICIoT2023, our CNN-GRU detects 90.4% of attacks, with 9.6% false alarms."

Do NOT say: "More training will fix it." Tuning and 3 seeds already showed no gain.
Q4: "Why is your CNN-GRU not better than a simple MLP?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"On per-packet features, all networks hit the same ceiling. MLP, 1D-CNN, GRU and CNN-GRU all score 0.858 over 3 seeds. XGBoost scores 0.868. A GRU helps when it sees a sequence over time. Here each row is one packet, processed alone. So the GRU has no real time dimension to use.

We report this openly. If the smallest model is needed today, the MLP at 24 KB is the honest choice. Our next step is to give the GRU real sequences: consecutive flow windows per device or connection. Then we can test whether the hybrid adds value."

Do NOT say: "The hybrid is the most accurate model." On our data it is not.
Q5: "Is SHAP trustworthy?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"Not by itself. That is why we tested it. We retrained without the top-5 SHAP features, and Macro-F1 fell from 0.858 to 0.605. We retrained without 5 random features, and it stayed at 0.858. So SHAP points to what the model really depends on.

We also learned that SHAP can mislead. Our early plots hid the "0" vs "0.0" shortcut, because they cut the suffix from feature names. One early plot even explained a model with the wrong inputs. Now training and SHAP refuse to run on mismatched data."

Q6: "Why a second dataset?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"Edge-IIoTset is single packets. Floods and scans are defined by packet rate, and one packet cannot show rate. Also, udp.time_delta is 0 for every attack row, so there is no timing. CICIoT2023 is flow-level, with rate and timing.

It also tested our method. On CICIoT2023 we found a second hidden shortcut: packet_count is the window size, 10 for Benign and Recon, 100 for DDoS, DoS and Mirai. We removed it. Results: binary 0.917 for XGBoost and 0.890 for CNN-GRU; 8 categories 0.752 and 0.675. The CNN-GRU category score goes from 0.34 to 0.68, and SHAP now uses rate. We have not yet done a cross-dataset test. That is a next step."

Q7: "Why is attack-type classification so weak on Edge-IIoTset?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"With honest strict features, 14 attacks plus Normal is hard from one packet. XGBoost gets 0.429 Macro-F1, and CNN-GRU gets 0.337 ± 0.033. We compared losses over 3 seeds: sqrt class weights 0.337, focal loss 0.254, cross-entropy 0.238. So sqrt weights work best; focal loss did not win. The main reason is the data: types like DDoS_UDP and Port_Scanning differ by rate, which a single packet cannot show. With flow data, CICIoT2023 categories reach 0.675 for our CNN-GRU."

Do NOT quote the old multiclass accuracy or Macro-F1 from before the fix. Those were measured on leaky data.
Q8: "Can it actually run on an IoT device?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"It is small enough. We measured on one CPU thread. With FP16 weights, the CNN-GRU is 159 KB, keeps 0.858, and takes 6.5 ms per packet, about 1,660 rows per second. INT8 on the GRU shrinks it to 88 KB but drops the score to 0.782, so we do not use it. The MLP is only 24 KB. XGBoost is faster on a CPU, 0.8 ms per packet, but 492 KB.

It would sit on the IoT gateway. We have not yet run it on a real Raspberry-Pi-class device. That is our next step."

Do NOT say: "sub-millisecond" or "already deployed on a Pi". Quote the measured numbers only.
Q9: "What is novel in your work?"
Honest Answer to Deliver

"Four things.
One: we found and removed hidden shortcuts in two public IoT datasets: the "0" vs "0.0" artifact in Edge-IIoTset and the window-size artifact in CICIoT2023. Both make models look perfect.
Two: we measured the detection ceiling of packet-level Edge-IIoTset, 67.5%, and showed our model reaches it.
Three: we proved explanation fidelity with the SHAP removal test, not just SHAP plots.
Four: an honest, reproducible evaluation: 3 seeds, a fair ablation against simpler models, validation-only thresholds, a tested edge model, and a GPU pipeline that runs on a laptop."

Do NOT say: "The CNN-GRU architecture is the novelty." Our ablation shows it gives no gain on packet data.
